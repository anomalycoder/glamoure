# UI/UX Pro Max Skill

## Purpose
Applies cognitive science, usability heuristics, and behavioral psychology to interface design, transforming disjointed screens into frictionless, intuitive user experiences.

## Core Directives

### 1. Nielsen's Usability Heuristics Applied
1. **Visibility of System Status**: Always provide instantaneous visual confirmation upon user action (e.g. appointment status badge transitions, subtle checkmark upon save).
2. **Match Between System and Real World**: Use familiar salon terminology (e.g. "Artisan Stylist", "Bay", "Ritual", "Walk-in Queue", "Booking Deposit").
3. **User Control and Freedom**: Clear "Cancel", "Back", or "Edit" escape hatches on multi-step booking and configuration flows without losing progress.
4. **Consistency and Standards**: Keep navigation bars, table action buttons, and modal dialog layouts in identical physical screen locations across Customer, Owner, and Member views.
5. **Error Prevention**: Validate dates, available working hours, and phone formats *before* submission, disabling invalid times automatically.
6. **Recognition Rather than Recall**: Display selected stylist, service price, and chosen date prominently during final confirmation rather than forcing users to remember earlier steps.
7. **Flexibility and Efficiency**: Provide keyboard shortcuts or single-click quick actions for power users (e.g., Quick Check-In from Owner Today's Schedule).
8. **Aesthetic and Minimalist Design**: Strip away extraneous decorative cards; display only what is actionable.
9. **Help Users Recognize and Recover from Errors**: Express errors in plain, compassionate language with a clear next step ("This time slot was just booked by another client. Please select 11:30 AM or choose another stylist.").
10. **Help and Documentation**: Contextual helper tooltips on complex salon settings (e.g., "Buffer minutes between appointments").

### 2. Laws of UX in Practice
- **Fitts's Law**: Place primary action buttons ("Book Appointment", "Confirm Booking", "Save Schedule") in thumb-accessible zones on mobile and consistent bottom-right or bottom-left on desktop dialogs.
- **Hick's Law**: Break the booking flow into progressive bite-sized steps (Service -> Stylist -> Time -> Details) instead of dumping 30 form fields on one page.
- **Miller's Law**: Group metrics and table columns into chunks of 5 to 7 items max.
- **Law of Common Region**: Group related salon staff metadata within subtle bordered cards.

### 3. UX Writing & Microcopy Standards
- **Buttons**: Verb + Noun (`Book Appointment`, `Confirm Reservation`, `Reschedule Visit`, `Save Changes`, `Approve Leave`).
- **No Robotic Marketing Slop**: Replace "Unleash the supreme power of your beauty journey" with "Your Scheduled Appointments".
- **Empty States**: Must explain what is missing, why it is empty, and provide an action button (`"No Upcoming Appointments" — "Explore our luxury rituals and book your next visit with master artisans." [Browse Services]`).
